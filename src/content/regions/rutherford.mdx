# Lede

When it comes to grapevines, Rutherford is one of Napa Valley’s big names. As a town, though, it is a blink-and-you’ll-miss-it spot along Highway 29 about halfway up the valley. You might catch the smoky aromas from Rutherford Grill without realizing you have passed through a community of only about 100 people. On Rutherford Cross Road, there is a small post office, the Rancho Caymus Inn and La Luna Market & Taqueria—a go-to for locally favored Mexican food. This sleepy appearance belies a rich wine history shaped by some of the region’s most important trailblazers.

The indigenous Mishewal-Wappo people first inhabited the land that now includes present-day Yountville, Oakville and Rutherford. In 1836, authorities granted nearly 12,000 acres on the valley floor to George C. Yount. In 1864, Yount gave his daughter Elizabeth 1,040 acres in the center of the modern-day AVA as a wedding gift when she married Thomas Rutherford. The Rutherfords planted vineyards and produced wine—and gave the town its name.

Finnish fur trader Gustave Niebaum acquired Inglenook in 1879 and built its semi-Gothic château in 1887; the estate still stands. French chemist Georges de Latour founded Beaulieu Vineyard on adjacent land in 1900 and hired André Tchelistcheff in 1938, launching one of the world’s most famous Cabernet programs. Rutherford sits at the widest point of Napa Valley, where benchlands divide into volcanic eastern slopes and cooler, windier western alluvial soils. Tchelistcheff’s phrase “Rutherford dust” captures the earthy, mineral lift that collectors chase in Cabernet from this stretch of road.

# Where to Taste

## Featured Wineries

### Cathiard Family Estate
- **Address:** 1978 Zinfandel Lane
- **Website:** cathiardvineyard.com

The late Daniel Cathiard built an extraordinary life in wine—Smith-Haut-Lafitte in Bordeaux, then this Mayacamas-footprint estate on the former Flora Springs property. Visits underline how deeply the family tends the place: intimate experiences might include vineyard tours in an electric Range Rover, the wine cave and on-site cooperage.

### Inglenook / Niebaum-Pennino
- **Address:** 1991 St. Helena Highway
- **Website:** inglenook.com

Even without the marquee name, Inglenook feels cinematic: history, beauty, Coppola memorabilia and polished wines. Experiences range from a simple glass to library tastings and tours. The Pennino House is a newer, living-room-elegant venue for relaxed flights and Italian-leaning non-alcoholic pairings.

### Quintessa
- **Address:** 1601 Silverado Trail
- **Website:** quintessa.com

An easy turn off the Silverado Trail leads to a world apart: nearly 300 acres centered on Dragon Lake. The “Quintessential Experience” places tastings in a private glass pavilion overlooking water and vines — a serene counterpoint to the Highway 29 bustle below.

### Staglin Family Vineyard
- **Address:** 1570 Bella Oaks Lane
- **Website:** staglinfamily.com

Staglin connects the modern Rutherford bench to Tchelistcheff-era history—and to families who never left. Vineyard walks explain why this site helped define “Rutherford dust,” and hospitality remains personal; second-generation vintner Shannon Staglin helps steer a winery that still feels like home.

# Where to Eat

## Rutherford Grill
- **Address:** 1180 Rutherford Road
- **Website:** rutherfordgrill.com

It would be easy to pigeonhole Rutherford Grill as the valley’s best barbecue-and-Cabernet pit stop—and the oak-grilled meats deserve that reputation. Dig deeper for deviled eggs, iceberg wedge, oak-grilled salmon and skillet cornbread in a room that stays friendly whether you sit at the bar or on the patio.

## La Luna Market & Taqueria
- **Address:** 1153 Rutherford Road
- **Website:** lalunamarket.com

This market and taqueria is where vineyard crews and vintners queue for tacos, burritos and aguas frescas. Order at the counter, grab picnic supplies for the road, and remember that some of the valley’s best meals are still served on paper plates.

## The Restaurant at Auberge du Soleil
- **Address:** 180 Rutherford Hill Road
- **Website:** auberge.com/auberge-du-soleil/dine

For a splurge above the fog line, Auberge’s flagship dining room pairs sweeping valley views with Mediterranean-focused wine country cooking and one of the deepest lists in Napa. Reserve well ahead—the terrace at golden hour is the textbook Rutherford luxury experience.

# Sidebar: The Sauvignon Blanc Discovery Tour

*A short companion itinerary to the Rutherford guide.*

**The Sauvignon Blanc Discovery Tour**

By MaryAnn Worobiec

Napa may be Cabernet country, but many red-wine estates also craft exceptional Sauvignon Blanc—and several worthy stops sit within a short drive of Rutherford. Start with a focused Sauvignon flight in Oakville, then explore St. Supéry’s Dollarhide-sourced whites before finishing with something crisp alongside Mustards Grill’s seafood tostada. It is a reminder that the valley’s “other” grape has as much terroir to express as the heavy hitters.
