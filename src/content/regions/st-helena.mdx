# Lede

St. Helena is the last substantial town before Calistoga, with Highway 29 running straight through its center. That can mean school-day traffic, but it also places you within walking distance of bakeries, tasting rooms, bottle shops and some of the valley’s most serious restaurants. The Wappo people were here first; Dr. Edward Bale’s 1840s land grant seeded the settlement Henry Still platted in 1854. By the late 19th century, Charles Krug’s first commercial bottling, White Sulphur Springs resorts and the arrival of the railroad had turned the community into Napa’s northern hub.

Prohibition forced a pivot to prunes, walnuts and other fruit; mature orchard trees still shade side streets. After Repeal, St. Helena became the valley’s everyday meeting point—winemakers still queue at espresso bars beside visitors in hiking boots. Ehlers Estate marks the geological pinch where the Mayacamas and Vaca ranges lean closest; Spring Mountain rises to the west with its own roster of appointments. Treat the town as a base camp: mornings on the bench, afternoons in the hills, nights along Main Street when locals reclaim the dining rooms.

# Where to Taste

## Featured Wineries

### Ad Vivum
- **Address:** 1400 Oak Avenue
- **Website:** advivumcellars.com

Chris Phelps brought Pétrus polish and Napa roots to this micro-production Cabernet project sleeping on Sleeping Lady Vineyard in Yountville. Visits are conversational and detail-rich—exactly what you want when the wine is built for two decades in bottle.

### Ehlers Estate
- **Address:** 3222 Ehlers Lane
- **Website:** ehlersestate.com

The 1886 stone winery still anchors tastings; veterans on the floor and winemaker Adam Casto often drift through. Partnerships with next-door Alila add food pairings but the spirit remains understated—polished without spectacle.

### Faust Haus
- **Address:** 2867 St. Helena Highway North
- **Website:** faustwines.com

The Huneeus family’s 1878 hospitality venue channels Belle Époque glamour: shops, small bites, library pours and a basement “speakeasy” lined with vinyl. Reserve for mountain-facing terrace seats when the light drops behind the Palisades.

### Spottswoode Estate Vineyard & Winery
- **Address:** 1902 Madrona Avenue
- **Website:** spottswoode.com

Few addresses telegraph “west St. Helena garden” like Spottswoode. The Novak family’s Cabernet and Sauvignon Blanc reflect some of the AVA’s most velvet tannins; tastings are appointment-only, mailing-list priority, adults only—plan months ahead.

# Where to Eat

## Charlie’s Napa Valley
- **Address:** 1327 Railroad Avenue
- **Website:** charliesnv.com

Elliott Bell’s French Laundry pedigree shows up in polished brasserie cooking—Little Gem salads, roast chicken, a late-night menu that fills with sommeliers and line cooks. The dining room feels like a supper club; the wine list leans California classic with Champagne depth.

## Cook St. Helena
- **Address:** 1310 Main Street
- **Website:** cooksthelena.com

Jude Wilmoth’s 2004 institution still hums with eggplant Parmesan, spicy sausage cavatelli and barrel-aged Negronis. Locals own the bar at 5 p.m.; the dining room’s closeness encourages table-hopping conversation Monday–Friday (lunch and dinner only).

## Celadon
- **Address:** 500 Main Street, Suite G
- **Website:** celadonnapa.com

Since 1996 this courtyard retreat has plated pork-belly lettuce cups, Moroccan lamb and wild-mushroom risotto with calm, confident service—useful when you need a quieter contrast to Main Street’s bustle.

## Breakfast, Coffee & Snacks

### Model Bakery
- **Address:** 1357 Main Street
- **Website:** themodelbakery.com

Karen and Sarah Mitchell’s oven turns out Oprah-famous English muffins, pastries and breakfast sandwiches worth the line. Order a sticky bun for the car; grab a loaf if your rental has a toaster.

# Sidebar: Four St. Helena Excursions

*Planning grid for readers who want routes, not randomness.*

**Four half-day itineraries**

By James Molesworth

Pair town appointments with Spring Mountain switchbacks or stay valley-bound: Seavey and Conn Valley reward early risers; Salvestrin and Corison keep you west of Highway 29; Freemark Abbey anchors a Judgment of Paris history day; Royal We and Wheeler Farms showcase modern salon hospitality. Leave slack for traffic heading south—the payoff is ending at Gott’s, the Charter Oak smash burger or Press with a bottle you picked up along the way.
