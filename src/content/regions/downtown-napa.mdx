# Lede

Once upon a time there were two Napas: the fashionable valley and the workaday seat of county government. That split has dissolved. Downtown Napa now holds more than forty tasting rooms—many walk-in friendly—alongside Grand Award dining, omakase counters, taco parlors and cocktail dens. The Oxbow Public Market, Copia’s reinvention as a Culinary Institute branch, First Street Napa’s mixed-use growth and the Rail Arts District’s mile of murals all speak to a city that no longer plays understudy to the vineyards upstream.

BottleRock, riverfront hotels and Food City’s indie cafes amplify weekend energy, yet weekday mornings still feel like a small Northern California town waking up—provided you know where to find the good espresso.

# Where to Taste

## Featured Wineries

### Gentleman Farmer Bungalow
- **Address:** 1564 First Street
- **Website:** gentlemanfarmerwines.com

Joey Wolosz and Jeff Durham converted a 1920s Craftsman into a living-room tasting space where library pours, family recipes and storytelling matter as much as the pour itself—an antidote to cookie-cutter salon design.

### Hestan Napa
- **Address:** 1300 First Street, Suite 291
- **Website:** hestan.com/hestan-napa

Stanley and Helen Cheng merged cookware emporium and Mark Dommen-led kitchen with their valley wines—think lunch tastings surrounded by gleaming pots, tech-forward appliances and big reds built for steak.

### Cadet Wine & Beer Bar
- **Address:** 930 Franklin Street
- **Website:** cadetbar.com

Dark, loud and deliberately urban, Cadet pours international bottles and cocktails beside grilled-cheese comfort—where industry folks steer visitors who want a break from polished château experiences.

### Mayacamas Downtown
- **Address:** 1256 First Street
- **Website:** mayacamas.com

If Mount Veeder feels too far, this salon distills Mayacamas tension and age-worthiness into a chic First Street room—current releases or library treats without the switchbacks.

# Where to Eat

## Angèle Restaurant & Bar
- **Address:** 540 Main Street
- **Website:** angelerestaurant.com

Since 2002 this gabled cottage has channeled French country cooking—onion soup, tartare, moules, croque monsieur—and a wine list split intelligently between California friends and French classics. The flowerpot of fries remains non-negotiable.

## La Toque
- **Address:** 1314 McKinstry Street
- **Website:** latoque.com

Ken Frank’s Grand Award dining room inside the Westin Verasa still spotlights luxury ingredients and an annual all-truffle menu that draws pilgrims. Choose the vegetarian or omnivore tasting and surrender to the pairings.

## Torc
- **Address:** 1140 Main Street
- **Website:** torcnapa.com

Creative small plates—sweet-potato pakora, black-truffle grilled cheese, inventive tasting menus—make Torc a goto when your group cannot agree on one cuisine. Staff sommeliers navigate the funkier pairings with a smile.

## Breakfast, Coffee & Snacks

### Moulin Bakery
- **Address:** 1321 First Street
- **Website:** moulinbakerycoffee.com

Follow the alley scent of butter and coffee for viennoiserie, galettes and espresso that rival anything up-valley—perfect before Oxbow browsing.

# Sidebar: Oxbow After Dark

*On the market that trained locals to stay downtown.*

**Public market rhythms**

By MaryAnn Worobiec

Ritual Coffee, Hog Island oysters, Gott’s, Live Fire and Loveski turn Oxbow into an all-day pantry; evenings mean wine bars, Fieldwork beer and the satisfaction of not driving back north after dessert. Pair a daytime tasting flight with a sunset piggyback through First Street—downtown Napa’s best trick is letting you go from casual to celebratory without a wardrobe change.
