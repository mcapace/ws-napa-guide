# Lede

Thirty miles north of downtown Napa, the valley tapers toward pine forest and Palisades basalt. Calistoga leans into that end-of-the-line geography: hotter days, cooler mountain winds at dusk, and a wellness economy that predates Cabernet tourism. Sam Brannan bottled Saratoga glamour into the town’s name; Indian Springs still steams with the same geothermal promise that drew Wappo sweat lodges centuries ago. Today’s Lincoln Avenue mixes mercantile storefronts, tasting rooms you can stroll between, chili-pepper spas and fine-dining resorts that pour mountain-grown wines as confidently as sparkling classics from Schramsberg.

Hourglass, Larkmead, Sterling’s gondola and Castello di Amorosa’s folly each tell a different story about volcanic soils. Visitors who embrace mud baths in the morning and Cabernet in the afternoon understand the town’s split personality—and its charm.

# Where to Taste

## Featured Wineries

### Larkmead Vineyards
- **Address:** 1100 Larkmead Lane
- **Website:** larkmead.com

Lillie Colt’s 1895 homestead became a Solari-family touchstone through Prohibition and into the modern era. Howard Backen’s tasting salon frames seated flights with vineyard walks and cheese pairings—an essential stop for anyone mapping Napa’s hottest bench.

### Schramsberg Vineyards
- **Address:** 1400 Schramsberg Road
- **Website:** schramsberg.com

Jack and Jamie Davies bet on sparkling wine in the 1960s when few Californians cared. Cave tours, historic bottles and sister-project Davies still wines keep appointments among the most educational effervescent experiences in the state.

### Lola Wines
- **Address:** 916 Foothill Boulevard
- **Website:** lolawines.com

Seth Cripe’s bungalow salon pours Russian River Pinot alongside Vermentino, Charbono and Counoise—proof that “Calistoga casual” can still geek out. Snacks, shaded patio seats and approachable pricing make it a weekday favorite.

### Hourglass
- **Address:** 4208 Silverado Trail
- **Website:** hourglasswines.com

Tony Biagi helps Jeff Smith and Carolyn Duryea translate Blueline’s Hobbit-door hillside into tense, polished reds. Post-Glass Fire rebuilds underscore the estate’s resilience; tastings thread cave and vineyard narrative with serious Bordeaux blends.

# Where to Eat

## Lovina
- **Address:** 1107 Cedar Street
- **Website:** lovinacalistoga.com

Plant-forward Cal-Italian cooking, Wagyu short-rib risotto and cheeky desserts anchor one of the liveliest dining rooms in town—vegans and carnivores actually share the same table without compromise.

## Calistoga Inn & Brewery
- **Address:** 1250 Lincoln Avenue
- **Website:** calistogainn.com

Riverfront patio, Napa Valley Brewing pints and wine-country comfort food—skirt steak, confit duck, wood-grilled pork—make this the default gathering spot after a dusty vineyard day.

## Picobar at Solage
- **Address:** 755 Silverado Trail North
- **Website:** auberge.com/solage/dine/picobar

Chef Gustavo Rios’s poolside Mexican kitchen trades on agave spirits, pristine tortillas and a wine list deep enough to forgive you for skipping a formal tasting-menu dinner.

## Breakfast, Coffee & Snacks

### Café Sarafornia
- **Address:** 1413 Lincoln Avenue
- **Website:** cafesarafornia.com

Since 1976 this diner has fueled hikers and hospitality crews with massive breakfasts—try the huevos rancheros before a Schramsberg tour.

# Where to Stay

## Featured Hotels

### The Francis House
- **Address:** 1403 Myrtle Street
- **Website:** thefrancishouse.com

Eight rooms in an 1886 Second Empire landmark: heated pool, salt room, sauna, clay court and interiors worthy of shelter magazines—proof that Calistoga can do intimate luxury without a corporate flag.

### Dr. Wilkinson’s Backyard Resort & Mineral Springs
- **Address:** 1507 Lincoln Avenue
- **Website:** drwilkinson.com

The 1952 roadside motor court grew into a Marriott-backed spa hotel where mineral pools, mud rituals and the House of Better restaurant lean into retro-camp aesthetics with modern mattresses.

### Solage, Auberge Resorts Collection
- **Address:** 755 Silverado Trail North
- **Website:** auberge.com/solage

Bungalows strung along shaded paths, a 20,000-square-foot spa—“mudslide” treatments included—and dual restaurants (Solbar + Picobar) let guests rotate between wellness and wine without starting the car.
